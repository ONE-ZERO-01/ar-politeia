\section{Observable definitions}
\label{sec:observables}

All definitions below are the exact functions in
\code{research/src/experiments/landscape\_study.py}. The density grid bins
particle positions onto the resource grid shape with the domain bounds.

\subsection{Resource--density Spearman correlation}
The primary observable is the Spearman rank correlation between the resource
field and the binned density field. Ties receive average ranks, and a constant
input is defined to have correlation zero. Physically, this measures whether
agents accumulate on high-resource cells: values near $+1$ mean dense cells are
resource-rich.

\subsection{Moran's I}
The global Moran's $I$ uses a symmetric four-neighbour lattice. For a centered
field $z$ with variance denominator $S$, it is
\begin{equation}
I = \frac{n}{w}\cdot\frac{\sum_{i\sim j} z_i z_j}{\sum_i z_i^2},
\qquad w = 2\bigl(R(C-1)+(R-1)C\bigr),
\end{equation}
where the numerator sums horizontal and vertical neighbouring products. It
measures spatial autocorrelation of density: positive values mean neighbouring
cells have similar occupancy.

\subsection{Occupancy entropy}
Occupancy entropy is the Shannon entropy of the normalized density mass,
divided by $\log(\#\text{cells})$, so it lies in $[0,1]$. It measures how
spread-out the population is: near $1$ means uniform occupancy, small values
mean concentrated occupancy.

\subsection{Wealth Gini}
The Gini coefficient is computed over non-negative wealth with the standard
mean-difference formula. It measures wealth inequality from $0$ (equal) to
$1$ (maximal concentration).

\section{Stationarity diagnostics}
\label{sec:stationarity}

For a steady-window sequence $y_1,\dots,y_m$, the diagnostic fits a linear
slope, normalizes the total window drift by $\max(|\bar y|,\mathrm{range}(y),10^{-12})$,
estimates the integrated autocorrelation time (IAT) with an initial-positive
autocorrelation sequence, and requires both
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{normalized\_drift} \le \mathrm{max\_normalized\_drift}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathrm{effective\_samples} = m/\mathrm{IAT} \ge \mathrm{min\_effective\_samples}.
\end{equation}
These are the preregistered thresholds in each job config.

\section{Proof sketches for executed checks}
\label{sec:proofs}

These sketches concern only what \code{E0-NUMERICS} actually executed and
passed; they are not asserted as general theorems beyond that regime.

\begin{sketch}[Total wealth conservation under the exchange rule]
For each interacting pair $(i,j)$, the transfer $\Delta w$ is added to $w_i$ and
subtracted from $w_j$ (or, in the OpenMP path, accumulated as $\pm\Delta w$ in
the two per-particle buffers). Therefore every pair contributes zero to the
sum of wealth. In \code{E0-NUMERICS} the production, consumption, and decay
rates are all zero, so the only wealth-changing process is the zero-sum
exchange; total wealth is conserved up to floating-point round-off. The
observed relative drift $2.511028287699446\times10^{-9}$ is within the
$10^{-8}$ threshold.
\end{sketch}

\begin{sketch}[Non-negativity in the tested regime]
For a single pairwise transfer, $|A_i-A_j|/(A_i+A_j)<1$ when both abilities
are positive, so $|\Delta w| < \eta\min(w_i,w_j)$ with $\eta=0.003$. Hence one
pairwise transfer cannot make either wealth negative when both are positive.
Because the code also guards $w\le0$ (skips such particles) and the observed
minimum wealth over all 25 runs is $5.1766245\times10^{-41}\ge -10^{-12}$, the
preregistered non-negativity check passes. This is an empirical statement for
the tested densities and step sizes, not a proof for arbitrary configurations
under the parallel accumulation scheme.
\end{sketch}

\begin{sketch}[Equal-wealth absorbing state]
If $w_i=w_j=w_0$ and $\epsilon_i=\epsilon_j$ (the equal-state conditions), then
$A_i=A_j$, so $\Delta w=0$ for every pair. The observed maximum wealth variance
under equal initial conditions is exactly $0.0$, within the $10^{-20}$
threshold. The uniform-wealth configuration is therefore an absorbing state of
the deterministic exchange kernel.
\end{sketch}
